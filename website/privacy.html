Privacy policy

This covers the Quiet Android app, the Quiet browser extension, and this website. The short version: nothing about you is collected, there is no account and no server, and the only thing that leaves your device is the network traffic the app or the browser was already making.

Last updated 28 September 2026, for version 1.0.7. The history of this page is the history of the repository, so a change here is visible rather than announced.

What is collected

  • Nothing. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no account, no telemetry, and no server either product reports to.
  • No identifier, no advertising ID, no fingerprint, no email address.
  • No page content, no browsing history, and no record of which app made a request.

What leaves your device

  • Android app: the DNS queries your device was making anyway, relayed to the resolver you chose, plus any blocklist download for a list URL you added yourself.
  • Browser extension: nothing at all. The lists are inside the package, so it makes no network requests at runtime.
  • This website: one request to GitHub's API for the current release version, and only if your browser runs the script for it. The page works without it.

What is stored, and where

  • Android app, on your phone: your settings and list files, a rolling seven-day log of blocked domain names, daily counters, and a salted hash of your PIN. The PIN itself is never stored.
  • Browser extension, in your browser profile: your settings, your allowlist, daily counters, and a salted PBKDF2 hash of your PIN.
  • Nothing is synced to us or to anyone else. Removing the app or the extension removes all of it, and the app also has a "clear statistics" control.

Why each permission is asked for

  • declarativeNetRequest — to block the domains in the lists that ship with the extension. It never reads a request: the rules are static files.
  • storage — to keep your settings, allowlist, PIN hash and counters on your device.
  • webNavigation — to count the page loads that were stopped and show that on the badge. No page content is read.
  • activeTab — to read the current tab's domain when you open the popup, so "Allow this site" refers to the page you are on.
  • Android's VPN permission — to filter DNS for every app, which is the app's only purpose. Only DNS is routed into the tunnel.

Remote code and third parties

  • Neither product loads or executes remote code. The extension ships its JavaScript, its rulesets and its icons, and nothing else.
  • No data is sold, shared, or transferred to anyone, because none is collected.
  • This site is served by GitHub Pages, which may keep access logs the way any web server does; those are not available to us and we have no analytics that would read them.
  • Downloads are served by GitHub Releases, so fetching one is a request from your browser to GitHub.

Children, and changes

  • The products collect no personal information from anyone, of any age.
  • They are not parental-control software: per-app rules, profiles and remote management are deliberately out of scope.
  • If this policy changes, the change is in this file's history in the public repository and in the release notes.
  • Questions, corrections or a problem with a store listing: open an issue at github.com/kennethyork/Quiet/issues.
